# Step 3: sale comps, 2026-10-30

How the sale value method was chosen and how well it does. Rerun with `python -m jobs.check_comps`.

## Pool

`python -m jobs.build_comps` builds 2,234 kept comps (191 whole buildings, 2,043 condo units) from the loaded Assessor sales, building characteristics and condo characteristics: first sale on or after January 1 of the year built, buildings of 2-6 units built 2014 or later. Dropped sales stay in the `comp` table with the reason: 168 multi-parcel sales, 112 Assessor sale filters, 89 deed type "Other", 16 under the price floor, 16 under half the community area median per sq ft. The 2026-10-29 reference pool (2,450) did not apply the deed type and Assessor filters, which is why it was larger.

## What was tested

Two measurements, each with the median ratio, COD and PRB the IAAO ratio study standard uses:

1. The 35 test buildings, each estimated without its own sale or anything within 0.02 mi.
2. An out-of-time holdout: the 283 pool sales from 2025-07-01 on, each estimated only from sales before it. This is the honest test: it shows the error on sales the method never saw, the way a user meets it.

Variants tried: FHFA index by tract, ZIP or metro; size adjustment for condos and buildings; 3, 4 and 5 year windows; an empirical-Bayes blend with k = 6 and 12; same-kind comps only; and a price factor for whole buildings estimated from condo comps.

What decided each choice (holdout median error unless noted):

| Choice | Result | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| Time index | metro 14.5%, tract 15.1%, ZIP 15.4%, none 16.2% | Metro. FHFA tract and ZIP indexes come from single-family loans and only 653 Cook tracts have a 2025 value. |
| Size adjustment | condo elasticity 0.50 helps (condo error 15.7% to 13.9%); building 0.37 raised test error 10.3% to 12.9% | Condo only. Planned buildings have default unit sizes, not measured ones. |
| Window | 3 years 12.5%, 4 years 13.7%, 5 years 14.0% | 3 years |
| Thin-area blend | k = 6: 13.9%, k = 12: 14.0%, rings: 14.0%; blend worsened building COD | Rings. The blend stays in the engine as an option. |
| Building factor | buildings estimated mostly from condo comps sold at 0.67 of the estimate | 0.70 on condo comps for whole-building estimates, fit on the 129 building sales before the holdout (grid 0.65-1.0), then checked on the holdout |

Against the 2026-10-29 method on the same pool, the holdout median error falls from 17.4% to 12.4% and the median ratio moves from 0.922 (estimates 8% low) to 1.000.

## What is still open

- Building COD: 61 holdout buildings with a COD of 24.8. Only 191 building sales exist since 2014, so most building estimates rest on condo comps. More building sales, or the Assessor characteristics of each building (bedrooms, baths), would help. Assessor `sv_is_outlier` flags (decision 9) come next.
- Regressivity: PRB -0.13. Common in comp medians (they pull toward the area middle). Next try: a price-tier split within each ring.
- The test set was used during the choice for the building factor's check, not its fit; the holdout and the fit set are separate.

# Fix the ranges before trusting the residual

Research date: 2026-10-30. Built from five research notes in `docs/research/research_notes/Methods review for V1 build/`. Figures marked "local" were computed on the repo's own data (`data/reference/new_construction_sales.csv`, `test_parcels.csv`, `permits_2to6_clean_city.json`) or on the spec's own defaults. They are not from a published source.

Most of the V1 method holds up against the literature and professional standards. The comp engine, the neighborhood-first fallback, the production cost preset, the residual land formula and the points-based score are all standard forms. The problems are in how the uncertainty around them is computed and labeled, and in a few specific bugs. Four findings move the answer the most. First, the sale range is a 50% band: the 25th-75th percentile of comps covered **17 of 35 test sales (49%)**. A calibrated 80% band from leave-one-out errors runs about **×0.74 to ×1.36** of the estimate. On a $925,000 3-flat that is $684,500 to $1,258,000. Second, the cost "P80" is mislabeled: AACE treats the high end of a range like ours as roughly a P90, so +30% is closer to P90 and the true P80 is about +19%. Third, residual land value is very sensitive. On our own defaults a **5% sale miss moves it 29%**, and a 5% sale miss plus a 5% cost miss moves it **52%**. That makes the 15% target return (13.0% of net sale, the low end of every benchmark found) a large lever: moving to 20% on cost cuts a stylized residual by **$50,457**. Fourth, the spec as written double-counts the neighborhood cost index. A Lincoln Park 3-flat would be costed at **$368 per sq ft instead of $250**. On the legal side, the Illinois Appraiser Act exempts *procuring* an automated valuation model (AVM) but says nothing on *providing* one, and its exemption section is scheduled for repeal on **January 1, 2027**. Both points need counsel. The 70%-within-15% sale target is reasonable. The current 69% (24 of 35) has a 95% confidence interval of 52% to 81%, though, and the full pool runs near 50%. So the target needs a second, harder test set beside it.

## Sale comps beat the literature's benchmarks on the test set, but a random new building is valued at about 15% median error

The method choices line up with the evidence. The IAAO AVM standard calls sales comparison "the preferred method of valuing all property" when sales exist. It then says comps "should be adjusted for attributes that are dissimilar to the subject," and that comp models backed by quantitatively derived adjustments are "more stable, reliable and statistically supported" ([IAAO AVM standard](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)). Our engine is an inverse-distance weighted median with no adjustments, so it meets the first test and fails the second. Bourassa, Hoesli and Peng found that appraiser-drawn neighborhoods beat statistical clusters as submarkets. A citywide model with sales-group dummies landed **46.0% within 10% and 75.0% within 20%**, against 35.0% and 63.3% with no submarkets ([Bourassa et al.](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/b023cc99-18b7-4f6b-8013-cbf6ce517016/download)). That supports Rule 4's Assessor-neighborhood-first fallback. In Louisville, 60 submarket dummies plus a spatial error term did best, at 47.4% and 75.2% ([Bourassa, Cantoni & Hoesli](https://www.prres.org/uploads/763/1688/Bourassa_Predicting_House_Prices_With_Spatial.pdf)). Gradient boosting was the most accurate learner on 123,000 Swiss sales, and robust regression was the most stable ([Mayer et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp1876.html)). On 8,249 Bay Area sales LightGBM reached 18.1% mean absolute percentage error against 26.5% for linear regression ([Bastos & Paquette](https://rem.rc.iseg.ulisboa.pt/wps/pdf/REM_WP_0314_2024.pdf)). The Cook County Assessor runs LightGBM on about 400,000 sales ([CCAO res model](https://github.com/ccao-data/model-res-avm)). LightGBM cannot show "the 6 comps behind this number," and Rules 2 and 5 require that. So it belongs in the background as a check model, and the displayed figure stays the adjusted comp estimate.

Our numbers, rerun this session (local), split sharply by test set. On the 35 permit-matched test buildings the spec method gives a **median ratio of 0.983, COD 14.2, PRD 0.997, median absolute error 7.8%, and 69% within 15%**. That reproduces the calibration doc's 24 of 35. Leave-one-out over the whole 2,450-sale pool is worse, at **14.9% median error**. Condo units land 45% within 15%, with a PRB of -0.29. That PRB is regressive: large units are underpriced and small units overpriced, which is what a median that ignores size does. Whole buildings show a COD of 38.9, dragged by very low recorded prices. The test set is easier than a random parcel. Note also that the spec's acceptance line still says "65% (24 of 37)." After the two removals on 2026-10-29 it should read 24 of 35.

For yardsticks, IAAO's COD band is **5-20 for 2-4 unit housing** and 5-15 for older or mixed single-family areas. It also wants a median ratio of 0.90-1.10, a PRD of 0.98-1.03 and a PRB within ±0.05 ([IAAO Standard on Ratio Studies](https://www.audits2.ga.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Standard_on_Ratio_Studies.pdf)). The county's own 2024 Chicago results show how hard our areas are. Hyde Park township ran a COD of 33.6 and Jefferson 22.2, against 9.2 in West Chicago ([CCAO 2024 Chicago report](https://prodassets.cookcountyassessoril.gov/s3fs-public/reports/cookCountyAssessor_annualReport_chicago-2024_v2_export-forWeb_20251020.pdf)). The township-to-number match came from flattened PDF text and should be rechecked before quoting. Freddie Mac's "Medium" confidence band is a forecast standard deviation of 13-20%, and its calibration test asks that at least 68% of "High" estimates land within ±13% on fresh sales ([Freddie Mac](https://my.sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/fact-sheets/dougwhitepaper_metricsmatter.pdf)). The 2024 federal AVM rule sets **no numeric thresholds**. It binds mortgage originators and secondary-market issuers, not tool vendors ([89 FR 64538](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/07/2024-16197/quality-control-standards-for-automated-valuation-models)). Against these, 70% within 15% is a fair internal target. Thirty-five buildings cannot separate methods that differ by less than about 15 points, though.

Five changes follow. The first is to adjust each comp before taking the weighted median. The time adjustment should use the FHFA tract, then ZIP, then metro index that `feasibility_defaults.json` already names. The spec's wording "adjusted to today with the sales trend" should be brought into line, because the pool's own median-by-year trend raised test error from **7.8% to 10.6%** (local). The IAAO standard says median-per-period trends do "not account for the myriad of other factors that affect sales prices" ([IAAO](https://www.iaao.org/wp-content/uploads/Standard_on_Automated_Valuation_Models.pdf)). Size per unit gets an elasticity measured within neighborhood-by-year groups: **0.50 for condo units and 0.37 for whole buildings** (local). A unit 10% larger sells for about 5% more. The median condo unit size in the Assessor data is 1,950 sq ft, which looks high and may include common area, so check it before relying on it. Kind (condo unit vs whole building) gets its own measured per-unit ratio. Comp age should be capped at five years, the IAAO limit for time-adjusted sales.

The second change replaces the hard fallback with an empirical-Bayes blend. The weight on the local median is n/(n+k), with k fit by leave-one-out and a start near 5-10. FHFA's "supertract" rule shows the same idea in practice: merge a thin tract with its neighbor until it reaches 40 half-pairs a year ([FHFA WP 21-01](https://fhfa.gov/document/wp2101.pdf)). The third change is the range. Show a calibrated 80% band from leave-one-out residuals, stratified by search ring and kind, in place of the 25th-75th percentile. Raw quantiles undercover: nominal 70/80/90% bands delivered 56/66/78% in Bastos and Paquette. Conformal calibration hit nominal coverage exactly ([Bastos & Paquette](https://rem.rc.iseg.ulisboa.pt/wps/pdf/REM_WP_0314_2024.pdf)). Our pooled 80% multipliers are ×0.74 / ×1.36 (local). By stratum, the 1.5-mile whole-building band runs **×0.35 to ×1.07**, so thin-data widening comes from the data itself. The fourth change adds the Assessor's sales-validation outlier flags. These sit in the public training-data parquet files, not in the Socrata sales dataset `wvhk-k5uv`. They match 1,938 of our 2,450 comps and flag only 10 ([CCAO sales-val](https://github.com/ccao-data/model-sales-val); local join). Keep our own "below 50% of comp median" pre-completion rule as well. The county's flags compare against all housing, and our whole-building pool has a 10th-percentile sale-to-estimate ratio of 0.44 (local). The fifth change uses Assessor certified value × 10 as a sanity flag at a 30% gap, after a backtest, and never blends it in. New homes show no value premium once size, location and date are controlled (about -1% to 0%), and the premium flips sign with the cycle ([Coulson, Morris & Neill](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Are-new-homes-special.pdf)). That supports using new-construction-only comps, as the pool already does.

## The $165 production preset survives four independent checks; the margins and the index rule do not

The production preset of $165/$170 per sq ft sits inside every public bracket found. NAHB's 2024 survey puts construction cost at **about $162 per sq ft** nationally ([NAHB 2024](https://nahb.org/-/media/AB4EFC742624475A97A0A62189986FF8.ashx)). ICC's August 2025 table gives **$155 for R-2 Type VB and $171 for R-3 VB**, before any regional factor ([ICC BVD](https://www.kirklandwa.gov/files/sharedassets/public/v/2/development-services/pdfs/building-pdfs/icc-august-2025.pdf)). The 2-flat permits come to $165 (local). The repo's Missing Middle back-out gives $162-$175. That last figure is an upper bound for a private builder. The program requires prevailing wage, 26% MBE and 6% WBE participation, and half of worker hours from Chicago residents ([Missing Middle guide](https://chicago.gov/content/dam/city/sites/land-sales/2025/Missing_Middle_Program_Guide_April2025_FINAL.pdf)). Terner put the prevailing-wage premium alone at about $30 per sq ft in California ([Terner Center](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Hard_Construction_Costs_March_2020.pdf)). The only free Chicago location factor found, 117.4 from 2011, is a labor premium: labor 150.27, materials 95.75 ([PNNL/Faithful+Gould](https://www.energycodes.gov/sites/default/files/2021-11/Location_Factors_Report.pdf)). ICC and NAHB figures times 1.174 give $182-$200, which matches the standard preset ($200/$215). Label standard "with third-party GC markup."

The margin bands should follow AACE's building-construction classes, as adapted from ASTM E2516-11 ([Alberta Infrastructure](https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/infra-tr-dt-series-19-estimates.pdf)). AACE says the ranges apply after contingency, around a point near the 50% confidence level ([AACE 18R-97](https://pdf4pro.com/view/18r-97-cost-estimate-classification-system-as-applied-2e8aba.html)).

| Our stage | AACE class | AACE building band | Current | Proposed | Dollar effect on $1,375,000 central |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feasibility | 5 | -20..-30 / +30..+50 | -20 / +30 | -20 / +35 until the contractor confirms presets | High side $412,500 → $481,250 |
| Layout | 4 | -10..-20 / +20..+30 | -15 / +20 | -15 / +25 | $275,000 → $343,750 |
| Drawings | 3 | -5..-15 / +10..+20 | -10 / +15 | no change | none |
| Bids | 2 | -5..-10 / +5..+15 | -5 / +5 | -5 / +10 | $68,750 → $137,500 |

The bid band at +5% is a Class 1 figure. Small-builder bids carry allowances and exclusions, so +10% fits better. The upward skew has wide support. The UK Green Book puts optimism bias for "standard buildings" between **2% and 24%** of capital cost ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-book-supplementary-guidance-optimism-bias)). Love et al. found overruns on 276 projects followed a heavy right-tailed Fréchet distribution, and contracts under A$1 million followed a Cauchy distribution ([Love et al. 2013](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/determining-the-probability-of-project-cost-overruns/)). A lognormal with sigma near 0.2 reproduces -20/+30 as P10/P90 (local). Under that reading P80 is **+19% at a +30% high and +22% at +35%**. The current `cost_confidence` label, "P80 = central × (1 + high margin)," therefore overstates the P80. Either compute P80 or rename the high end "high case." AACE's exact confidence level for the range could not be confirmed from a primary text.

The permit cost index needs a different stated reason and a fix to how it is applied. Chicago's fee calculator uses construction type, occupancy, floor area and scope, with no reported cost ([City fee calculator](https://chicago.gov/city/en/depts/bldgs/provdrs/permits/svcs/permit_fee_calculator.html)). So the spec's explanation, "reported costs run low because permit fees are based on them," is wrong for new construction. **69% of the 836 reported costs are exact multiples of $50,000** (local). That points to rounded guesses. The 3-flat median ($450,000, the same as the 2-flat median on 49% more floor area) comes to **$111 per sq ft**, about 33% below production. Use the index only as a coarse ranking, at half weight, within unit-count groups, as v2 already does. The bigger problem is double counting. The spec and the JSON apply `1 + 0.5 × (index − 1)` to every preset. The same index also selects the high preset, so Lincoln Park (index 1.94) prices at **$250 × 1.47 = $368 per sq ft**. The calibration scripts apply the adjustment only to production, capped at index 1.3, and use 1.0 for standard and high (`scripts/research/2026-10-29/cal.py` line 26, `run10.py` line 60). Write that calibrated rule into the JSON and spec before the engine copies the other one.

Housing finance agencies require at least **5% hard-cost contingency** on new construction. They cap A/E near 3.3% of total development cost and builder profit, overhead and general requirements at 6% / 2% / 6% ([NM MFA 2025](https://housingnm.org/uploads/documents/2025_Universal_Multifamily_Initial_Underwriting_Supplement_11.07.2024.pdf)). Keep 5% inside the central figure at every stage, since AACE counts contingency as expected spend. Split the 22% soft cost into named, overridable lines that sum to 22% until the contractor supplies real figures. Those lines are A/E, survey and soils, insurance, legal and closing, and GC general requirements when a third-party GC is used. The current escalator, FRED WPUIP2311001, covers goods only and "exclude[s] labor" ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WPUIP2311001); [NAHB RIPI](https://eyeonhousing.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/RIPI_Methodology_Document-FINAL.pdf)). Switch to the Census constant-quality index of single-family houses under construction, which excludes land ([Census](https://census.gov/construction/cpi/pdf/comparisonofpriceindexes.pdf)). Add escalation to the construction midpoint, about 8-9 months out, as its own line. At the **3.7%-5.9% input inflation of April 2026** ([NAHB](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2026/05/building-material-prices-increase-at-fastest-pace-in-three-years)) that adds about 2.5%-4%. Keep the Homes England rule to refit bands from actual P10/P90 once 20 or more jobs are complete.

## Two 5% errors move residual land value by half, so the target return and finance math carry real weight

The residual structure is the standard one: gross development value minus build cost, fees, finance and developer return ([GOV.UK PPG Viability](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/viability)). PPG also says "the price paid for land is not a relevant justification" for missing policy. That matches Rule 5: the residual comes from sale value and cost, never from an asking price. One formula detail is off. Finance is charged on cost that includes land, so the spec's `net sale / (1 + r) − (total cost − land)` is circular. The exact solve is **L = net sale / ((1 + r)(1 + f)) − C0**. C0 is non-land cost before financing. f is the finance-and-carry factor, 0.0771 on the spec defaults. For the standard Humboldt Park 3-flat the spec gives **−$160,742 and the exact solve −$144,941**, a gap of $15,800, or 1.7% of net sale (local).

Sensitivity is the bigger story. On a stylized parcel (net sale $1,500,000, non-land cost $1,000,000, residual $210,981 at a 15% target), each $1 of net sale moves the residual $0.81 and each $1 of cost moves it $1.00 (local):

| Change | Residual | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Sale −5% | $150,432 | −28.7% |
| Cost +5% | $160,981 | −23.7% |
| Sale −5% and cost +5% | $100,432 | −52.4% |
| Target 10 / 15 / 20 / 25% on cost | $266,026 / $210,981 / $160,524 / $114,103 | |

The target return is the largest single editable lever. Our 15% on cost equals **13.0% of net sale**. UK planning guidance treats **15-20% of GDV** (17.6-25% on cost) as a suitable return ([PPG para 019](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/viability)). Research for RICS found residential developers commonly use **20% on cost for low-risk sites with planning permission and 25% for higher-risk sites** ([RICS Land Journal](https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/land-journal/approaches-to-developer-returns-in-appraisals.html)). NAHB builders report profit of 11.0% of price, about 12.4% on cost after overhead ([NAHB](https://eyeonhousing.org/2025/01/cost-of-constructing-a-home-in-2024/)), and a gross margin of 20.7% ([NAHB CODB](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2025/04/nahbs-new-study-provides-statistics-and-data-on-builder-financial-performance)). Most V1 parcels are unpermitted, which RICS puts in the higher-risk group. So 15% is a low-end reference. Show 15 / 20 / 25% on cost as labeled reference lines, with on-net-sale figures beside them. The default is Marlon's call.

On finance, the simple-average draw method is accepted practice. A symmetric S-curve averages 50-53% drawn ([APERS](https://apers.app/learn/capital-structure/senior-debt/construction-loans-draw-schedules-interest-reserves)), so 55% is reasonable with land and site work funded early. The 8% rate sits inside 2026 bank construction pricing of **SOFR (about 3.64%) plus 275-400 bp, or 7.00-8.75%** ([CORE Advisors](https://www.coradvisors.net/2026/05/construction-loan-rates-2026.html), a secondary aggregator). Three items are missing. The first is land carry during design and permit review. The defaults already measure that review at a median of 50 days (self-certified) or 85 days (standard), but the finance formula never uses it. The second is a loan origination fee. A 1% default is unverified and needs a lender quote. The third is a sell-period balance that matches the exit: 100% outstanding for a whole-building sale, about 50% for condo closings that pay the loan down. A six-month delay on the Humboldt Park 3-flat costs about **$23,600 of interest plus $6,800 of tax and insurance** (local).

The hold scenario's 0.65 operating factor likely overstates net operating income (NOI) on new Chicago buildings. Chicago's effective tax rate on a median homestead was **1.52%** ([Civic Federation](https://civicfed.org/node/4141)). At a 6.25% cap rate, taxes alone take **24% of NOI**, or about 16% of effective gross income. At 2.0%, plausible for a non-homestead rental, they take 32% of NOI. That leaves too little for insurance, repairs, management and reserves (local). The Cook County Assessor puts taxes inside expense ratios and describes a "loaded" rate that adds the effective tax rate to the cap rate ([CCAO cap rate policy](https://prodassets.cookcountyassessoril.gov/s3fs-public/event/Assessor%20Cap%20Rate%20Policy%20(FINAL)%204.24.2023.pdf)). Use value = NOI before tax / (cap + effective tax rate). That shows tax as its own sourced line and removes the circular reference. The 6.25% cap sits inside 2025 broker figures of 5.5-6.0% ([Northmarq](https://www.northmarq.com/sites/default/files/docs/NMMarketInsights-Q42025-Chicago.pdf)) and 6.7% ([Matthews](https://www.matthews.com/market_insights/chicago-il-multifamily-market-report-q4-2025)). The two disagree by 70-120 bp, and neither splits out 2-6 unit buildings. Show the comp-based sale value beside the income value for 2-4 units, since the likely buyer is an owner-occupant. Also show a DSCR-sized permanent loan (NOI / (1.25 × mortgage constant), about 10.55 × NOI at 6.5% over 30 years) against the construction payoff.

The existing-use check follows practice. PPG sets the land benchmark at existing-use value plus a premium ([PPG](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/viability)). Dye and McMillen found Chicago teardown prices "approximately equal" land value ([Dye & McMillen 2007](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/teardowns-and-land-values-in-the-chicago-metropolitan-area/)). Clapp et al. show the residual ignores redevelopment option value. In their model the true land share near teardown was 0.8, but the residual gave 0.57 ([Clapp, Cohen & Lindenthal 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11146-021-09834-4)). Show two numbers beside the residual: Assessor market value, and the nearby teardown and vacant-sale median with its count and dates. Lot price rises less than one-for-one with lot area: McMillen and Singh's coefficient is 0.858 ([Lincoln Institute](https://www.lincolninst.edu/app/uploads/2024/04/singh_and_mcmillen_land_value_estimation_using_teardowns.pdf)). Per-buildable-sq-ft pricing helps. Report vacant and teardown medians separately before pooling them. FHFA tract land prices make a free third fallback ([FHFA WP 19-01](https://fhfa.gov/research/papers/wp1901)).

Incentive timing matters most for Missing Middle. The grant is paid as a reimbursement after buildings are complete. Sale prices must be affordable at **140% AMI** to owner-occupants ([Missing Middle guide](https://chicago.gov/content/dam/city/sites/land-sales/2025/Missing_Middle_Program_Guide_April2025_FINAL.pdf)). Adding $150,000 per unit to a comp-priced sale overstates profit. The program's net effect is grant + land saving − (comp price − capped price) × units, with the grant as a receipt at completion. The Opportunity Zone dates in the spec ("2018 list valid through 2028") need restating. Under the 2025 federal act, new investments in old zones end December 31, 2026, and new zones start January 1, 2027 ([BNN CPAs](https://www.bnncpa.com/resources/qualified-opportunity-zone-enhancements-and-expansion-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-obbba/), a secondary source). Bonus depreciation should show its undiscounted first-year deferral, which meets Rule 6, plus a present-value line, outside profit. The Enterprise Zone exemption should reduce hard cost before financing. AHSAP needs 7+ units and the ARO triggers at 10+ ([CCAO](https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/affordable-housing); [City ARO](https://chicago.gov/city/en/depts/doh/provdrs/developers/svcs/aro.html)), so both stay out of 2-6 unit scenarios. The current CSV already leaves them out.

## Skipping setbacks costs at most 5% on standard lots; parking and the administrative adjustment cost whole units

Published capacity models take the most restrictive of four caps: units per lot, density × area, FAR massing, and height × coverage after setbacks ([Terner Housing Policy Simulator](https://www.ternerlabs.org/terner-housing-policy-simulator-full-methodology)). Ours uses density and FAR. The code arithmetic says that is sound for interior standard lots ([Chicago Zoning 17-2-0300](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/chicago/latest/chicagozoning_il/0-0-0-48935)). On a 25 × 125 lot, FAR binds before setbacks in RS-3 (2,812 sq ft allowed vs about 3,500 of envelope) and RT-4 (3,750 vs 4,500). In RM-5 the envelope (about 6,000 sq ft) sits **about 4% below FAR** (6,250). It rises back to 6,400 under the 24% rear setback for buildings with 33% accessible units (local arithmetic). Keep the V1 method. Add an envelope cap as a check that flags when it falls below FAR. Corner, shallow, irregular and B/C lots will show larger errors, and those setbacks were not checked. Zoned capacity is also an upper bound in practice. In Los Angeles about **1.2% of parcels developed in five years** ([Terner Center](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/research-and-policy/stronger-housing-element-los-angeles/)). That supports showing capacity as math and not as a recommendation.

The administrative adjustment path is the largest open zoning question. The code text found says the Zoning Administrator may allow development on a lot that fails "the minimum lot area standards of Sec. 17-2-0301-A." It does not mention lot area per unit ([17-13-1003-A](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/chicago/latest/chicagozoning_il/0-0-0-51818)). Chicago Cityscape treats the adjustment as a 10% cut in lot area per unit. Its "Standard 8-3" floor of 2,880 sq ft is 90% of 8 × 400 ([Chicago Cityscape](https://publish.obsidian.md/chicagocityscape/standard6383)). Our engine follows Cityscape. On a 25 × 108 RT-4 lot it shows 3 units by adjustment. Under the code text it stays at 2. Until a zoning attorney or DPD guidance settles it, label the path "practice, see source" and cite both. The +10% height adjustment is conditional for new buildings: no taller than buildings on abutting lots (17-13-1003-L).

Parking is wrong in the engine today. The engine uses 1 space per unit outside transit-served locations (TSLs) and 0 inside any TSL. The code sets **2 per unit for RS-3 detached houses and 1.5 for RS-3 two-flats**, with no parking for houses and two-flats on lots of record 33 ft wide or less without an improved alley ([17-10-0207](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/chicago/latest/chicagozoning_il/0-0-0-50685)). Ordinance O2025-0015577 removed parking minimums by right in TSLs, effective 2025-09-25 and covering 74% of the city. It still requires an administrative adjustment to go below 50% where a lot is transit-served only through a Metra station ([Taft Law](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/chicago-eliminates-parking-requirements-for-transit-served-locations/); [Chicago Cityscape](https://www.chicagocityscape.com/blog/chicago-eliminates-parking-requirements-across-most-of-the-city-8d87a6b21f)). Put the district table into data. Show Metra-only lots at 50% by right. American Legal's 17-10-0102-B still shows the older text, so cite the ordinance.

Several smaller parcel-data choices should change. Frontage from the short side of the oriented bounding rectangle is exact on rectangular interior lots. It is wrong on wide-shallow lots, where it reports depth as frontage, and ambiguous on corner lots. Frontage sets height at the 32-ft break in RM-4.5/RM-5 and the parking exemption at 33 ft. A street-aware method using the address street's centerline (pr57-gg9e) should replace it, with the bounding-box value kept as a fallback. Zoning should go to the district with the largest area share, not the district under the point-on-surface. Keep the top two shares and ignore slivers under about 50 sq ft. The National Zoning Atlas cleans slivers before analysis ([NZA](https://www.zoningatlas.org:443/how)). The RS-3 two-flat test measures "the same side of the block." The hundred-block proxy can merge two block faces or split one. Use centerline segments instead, and settle whether the code counts lots or buildings: the American Legal summary says lots and Cityscape quotes buildings ([Cityscape](https://www.chicagocityscape.com/blog/restrictive-single-family-only-zoning-district-actually-allows-2-flats-under-extreme-conditions-1dbe9d3cfef0)). The same block-face structure drives ADU caps of 1/2/3 per year in RS-1/2/3. RS ADUs need an administrative adjustment ([Zoneomics mirror, 17-7](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/chicago-IL/chapter_7)), which may be out of date. For violations and 311 calls, the 15 m nearest-lot rule spans about two 25-ft (7.6 m) lots on each side. Match by address first and store the match method. Existing-unit counts need three data checks: sum apartments across all cards (coach houses), drop condo parking and storage PINs, and confirm the 313-399 class list. The code also leaves out Type A ground-floor exclusions and the 33% conversion-unit bonus in 17-2-0303.

## Labeled numeric ranges keep trust; corner arithmetic, double-counted points and unlabeled estimates are the exposures

How ranges are shown matters as much as how they are computed. In five UK experiments with 5,780 people, a numeric range caused a small drop in trust in the number and **no significant change in trust in the source**. A verbal hedge cost more on both counts ([van der Bles et al. 2020](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/the-effects-of-communicating-uncertainty-on-public-trust-in-facts/); effect sizes from a [secondary summary](https://blog.aiimpacts.org/p/paper-summary-the-effects-of-communicating)). Their earlier review reports that about half of readers take the **highest value in a range** as the right figure ([van der Bles et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952)). For buyers deciding what to pay, that is the costly direction. Adding a central estimate and simple graphics narrows how differently people read a range ([Dieckmann, Peters & Gregory 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/riskan/v35y2015i7p1281-1295.html)). EFSA's guidance gives four rules. State the probability a range covers. Say "central," not "best." Keep the central figure visually distinct. Use box plots over density charts ([EFSA 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7292191/table/nlm-table-wrap-1)). Non-appraisers anchor on a published value and expert appraisers do not ([Cypher & Hansz 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jpropr/v20y2003i4p305-318.html)), so comp count belongs next to every number. This supports Rule 3's dollar format with three additions: a bold central figure, a fixed probability label on each range, and a separate evidence line with numbers, such as "9 sales within 1.5 mi, Mar 2022-Aug 2026; range widened 10 points: 6 permits in this area." Zillow and Redfin publish median error and within-X% shares. Redfin's off-market estimate lands **59.99% within 10% and 81.72% within 20%** ([Redfin](https://redfin.com/redfin-estimate)). We should publish the same table by community area.

The profit range today is corner arithmetic: low sale minus high cost, to high sale minus low cost. It mixes a 50% sale band with an undefined cost band, assumes the worst of both happen together, and hides cost skew. On the stylized parcel the low corner is **−$265,650**. A 100,000-draw simulation gives a P10 of **−$122,632**, a P50 of $174,962 and a 23% chance of loss (local). A second illustration found median profit about **$40,000 below "central minus central"** because cost skews high. Modest positive sale-cost correlation (0.3-0.6) narrowed the band (local; assumed inputs). The sale spread in those runs (sigma about 0.15) is narrower than our data supports. The pooled 80% band of ×0.74 / ×1.36 implies a sigma near 0.24 (local), so a data-fit simulation will show a wider profit range and a higher chance of loss. Evans applied Monte Carlo to the residual method in 1992 ([Evans](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/09588689210033846/full/html)). Iman-Conover is the standard way to correlate inputs with arbitrary distributions ([Iman & Conover 1982](https://ftp.math.utah.edu/pub/tex/bib/idx/communstatsimulcomput1980/11/3/311-334.html)). V1 should run 10,000 seeded draws per scenario in the engine, so the nightly score and the live screen agree. Report P10/P50/P90 and the share of draws with a loss. Keep a three-row scenario table, label the corner "stress case," and add a 5 × 5 sale-by-cost grid in dollars, with rows for target return and a six-month delay.

The points score is a standard weighted linear combination, which is defensible for GIS site selection when it is transparent and validated. Weights are "the most subjective component" of such models. Ligmann-Zielinska and Jankowski split sites into robust ones (high mean, low spread across sampled weights) and candidates whose rank depends on the weights ([Ligmann-Zielinska & Jankowski 2014](https://project.geo.msu.edu/stsa/pubs/EMS2014_reprint.pdf)). Our score double-counts market strength. Strong area sales earn location points and also raise the profit points, and more by-right units raise both zoning-upside and profit points ([development_score.json](../../../data/rules/development_score.json)). Profit points also ignore evidence quality: 40 comps at 0.75 mi and 6 comps at 3 mi score the same. Drop or rework `area_sales_12m_above_city_median`. Base profit points on the P25 or P10 return. Then backtest point-in-time: rebuild inputs as of 2018-2021, use a 1-6 unit new-construction permit within 36 months as the outcome, and compare against random, zoning-only and vacant-only baselines by decile lift and precision@k. Hold out by time and by community area. The 35 test parcels are too few to validate a citywide ranking. "Was developed" measures where developers built. It does not measure profit, and a score tuned to it will repeat past patterns.

The legal exposure is narrow but real. Illinois defines appraisal as "the act or process of developing an opinion of value" ([P.A. 100-0604](https://ilga.gov/legislation/publicacts/100/100-0604.htm)). §5-5(h) says the Act "does not apply to procuring an automated valuation model," and §5-5(c) exempts a broker's BPO or CMA under the Real Estate License Act. The same page shows §5-5 **scheduled for repeal January 1, 2027** ([225 ILCS 458/5-5](https://ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/022504580K5-5.htm)). In Patel v. Zillow the Seventh Circuit found no private right of action. It held that values "labeled … as estimates" are opinions, because the deceptive-practices act "prohibits deception rather than error" ([Patel v. Zillow](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-7th-circuit/1980489.html)). The practical defense is labeling and published accuracy. Use "Estimate from public records and software. No property was inspected. Not an appraisal, and not zoning, legal, tax or investment advice." Never use "appraised," "certified" or "valuation report." Bar credit-decision use in the terms, because lenders who rely on us inherit the AVM rule's five quality-control factors, nondiscrimination among them ([89 FR 64538](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/07/2024-16197/quality-control-standards-for-automated-valuation-models)). Any public accuracy claim needs the dated backtest on file ([FTC substantiation policy](https://www.ftc.gov/node/52431)). A broker who hands a client a 25x125 value for a fee outside a commission may be producing a BPO. Counsel should review both that and the procure-versus-provide gap.

Fairness needs measuring, because the comps carry the market's patterns. Berry's 2025 evaluation of Cook County found regressivity cut sharply: the bottom-5% vs top-5% assessment ratio fell from 153% to 116%. COD stayed near 19 ([Berry 2025](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/voices.uchicago.edu/dist/6/2330/files/2025/09/Kaegi-Evaluation-9_04.pdf)). Appraisals came in below contract **7.4% of the time in white tracts, against 12.5% in majority-Black and 15.4% in majority-Latino tracts** ([Freddie Mac 2021](https://www.FreddieMac.com/fmac-resources/research/pdf/202109-Note-Appraisal-Gap.pdf)). Homes in 50%-Black neighborhoods listed **about 23% lower per sq ft** after controls ([Brookings 2018](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2018.11_Brookings-Metro_Devaluation-Assets-Black-Neighborhoods_final.pdf)). Our method can add to that in two places. Wide fallback rings fire in thin South and West Side markets. And the location points reward areas already above the median. Run leave-one-out sales-ratio statistics monthly by community area, tract racial majority (ACS), search ring and price decile. Report the median ratio, COD, PRD and within-10/20% shares. Mark areas with fewer than 6 sales as unmeasured. Audit the score by the same groups with and without location points. Never use race as a model input, and never adjust prices for it.

## Conclusion

The review shifts where the risk sits. The method choices Marlon has made are mostly the ones the literature and the Cook County Assessor would make. The weak points are calibration and labeling, and those decide whether a user trusts the max land price. A residual that moves 52% on two 5% errors cannot carry a 50% sale band and an over-labeled P80 without misleading people. The fixes all come from data already in the repo: leave-one-out residuals for sale bands, the lognormal reading of the AACE classes for cost, and a seeded simulation in the engine. The 2,450-sale pool is more useful as a calibration set than as a comp pool, because it shows honest error in the parcels users will actually search, at about 15% median error, where the hand-picked set shows 8%.

The second shift is that two fixes change results by more than any method upgrade: the cost-index double count (up to $118 per sq ft on Lincoln Park) and the Missing Middle price cap. Both are data edits under Rule 1. Neither needs research. The open items that do need outside input are few. They are the default target return, the administrative-adjustment reading of 17-13-1003-A, the AVM licensing gap and the 2027 sunset, a lender quote for rate and fees, and the contractor's confirmation of presets and the soft-cost split.
